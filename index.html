ABOUT THE WORKSHOP

A changing landscape of energy vulnerability

Energy poverty and energy vulnerability remain significant global challenges, with far-reaching consequences for health, economic stability, social inclusion, and inequality. At the same time, the clean energy transition is changing the drivers and patterns of energy vulnerability, creating new forms of inequality.

The challenge is therefore to understand how vulnerability emerges and persists, how energy markets and climate-related shocks reshape these risks, which new inequalities arise from technological and policy transformations, and how innovation and economic policy can support a more inclusive energy transition.

This workshop seeks to advance this research agenda through microdata, longitudinal and causal evidence, novel data sources, economic modelling, and innovative analytical approaches.

CALL FOR PAPERS

Topics of Interest

We invite original contributions addressing emerging and persistent dimensions of energy vulnerability.

01

Measuring & understanding energy vulnerability

Novel data and analytical methods to capture hidden vulnerability, heterogeneity and dynamics beyond conventional indicators.

02

Energy market transformation

Pricing mechanisms, decentralised generation and new consumer–prosumer relationships reshaping household vulnerability.

03

Long-term & cumulative consequences

How energy deprivation affects physical, mental and social health outcomes.

04

Extreme climate events & resilience

Heatwaves, cold spells, floods, storms and other climate-related shocks, and household, infrastructure and policy resilience.

05

Evidence-based policy responses

Effectiveness, targeting, distributional impacts and unintended consequences of social tariffs, subsidies, efficiency programmes and carbon-pricing compensation.

06

Technology & energy inequality

Whether access to renewable energy, smart grids, storage and other clean technologies reduces energy poverty or creates new inequalities.

07

Energy justice in the transition

How costs, benefits, opportunities and risks are distributed across households and population groups, with attention to vulnerable populations.
